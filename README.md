What are the differences, what are things we should research, what skills would we be using: Project Problem Statement




Between 2012 and 2024, social media transformed from an emerging communication tool into a powerful force shaping journalism, politics, culture, and public life. Over nearly a decade, the Cathy Hughes School of Communications hosted the annual Social Media Technology Conference and Workshop, bringing together researchers to document this digital shift. However, the vast body of academic knowledge produced during this period currently remains scattered, unstructured, and noisy. Culled from Easy Chair conference software across discontinuous years (2012–2019, 2021, and 2024), critical research is trapped inside fragmented abstracts, paper submissions, presentations, programs, and workshop recordings.




Without an efficient way to search and synthesize this longitudinal record, researchers face significant friction attempting to analyze how scholarship evolved over time. This creates severe knowledge bottlenecks—ranging from mild inefficiencies in locating past conference materials to the devastating loss of institutional memory, obscuring historical insights on overlooked communities and masking how early predictions compared against the rise of modern AI algorithms. Our product aims to provide an AI-driven research platform that turns this decade-long archive into a structured, searchable, and verifiable record of change.





Project Solution Description




Our product is the Social Media Research Navigator, an intelligent AI agent designed to turn ten years of scattered, noisy, and unstructured conference materials into an accessible, searchable, and verifiable system of record. Developed for the Cathy Hughes School of Communications, the application ingests academic submissions, abstracts, workshop files, and recordings collected from Easy Chair software spanning 2012 to 2024.




When queried, the Social Media Research Navigator acts as an autonomous analytical intermediary between the user and the raw archive. It automatically traces emerging research themes, highlights overlooked communities, and retrieves the verifiable original source document behind every answer without requiring tedious manual file searching. To provide deep longitudinal insight, the platform incorporates a dedicated “Then and Now” feature that dynamically compares early academic predictions regarding platform adoption against later empirical evidence and modern AI algorithms. By automating archive retrieval and factual verification, our application preserves vital institutional knowledge while equipping scholars with practical capabilities in research ethics, data analysis, and communication tech history.
